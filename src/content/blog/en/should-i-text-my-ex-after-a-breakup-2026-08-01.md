## The Urge to Text: Why It's So Strong

It's 11 p.m., and your thumb hovers over the keyboard. You've typed and deleted the same message a dozen times. *Should I text my ex?* It's a question that plagues almost everyone after a breakup. Your mind races with "what ifs," and the loneliness feels deafening.

First, take a deep breath. You're not weak for wanting to reach out. You're human. Breakups trigger a literal withdrawal in the brain, similar to addiction. That urge to text is a craving for the comfort you once knew. But acting on it can often set your healing back.

Before you hit send, let's walk through some essential questions to ask yourself.

## 7 Questions to Ask Yourself Before Texting Your Ex

### 1. What is my true intention?

Be brutally honest. Are you texting to:

- Get them back?
- Seek closure?
- Ease your loneliness?
- Make them jealous?
- Just check in?

If your goal is to rekindle the relationship, ask yourself: *Has anything truly changed?* If you're seeking closure, remember that closure is something you give yourself, not something they provide. If you're lonely, a text will only offer a temporary fix, not a cure.

### 2. How will I feel if they don't reply?

Imagine sending that message and watching the "Delivered" receipt turn into "Read"... with no response. How would that feel? If the thought alone makes your stomach drop, you're not ready to send. Your emotional state is too fragile, and their silence could send you into a spiral.

### 3. Have I given myself enough time to heal?

The first few weeks after a breakup are a storm of emotions. Your judgment is clouded by grief, anger, and longing. Sending a text in this state is like making a life decision while sleep-deprived. Experts often suggest a no-contact period of at least 30-60 days. This isn't a punishment—it's a gift of clarity.

### 4. What do I hope to gain, realistically?

Let's say they reply. What will that actually give you? A fleeting dopamine hit? A few minutes of conversation that ends with you feeling emptier? Or maybe it turns into a painful back-and-forth that reopens the wound. Weigh the potential short-term comfort against the long-term pain.

### 5. Am I respecting their boundaries?

If they asked for space, texting them breaks that boundary. If the breakup was their decision, they may need distance to move on. Respecting their space is also a form of self-respect. You deserve to be with someone who chooses you, not someone you have to convince.

### 6. What would I tell my best friend?

Imagine your best friend is in your exact situation. They ask, "Should I text my ex?" What would you say? You'd probably hug them and say, "No, honey. Let's get ice cream instead." Be that friend to yourself.

### 7. Is there an alternative?

Instead of texting your ex, what else could you do? Call a friend. Write a letter you don't send. Take a walk. Journal your feelings. Or talk to an AI therapist—someone who can listen without judgment, 24/7. These actions move you forward instead of keeping you stuck.

## The No-Contact Rule: Why It Works

You've probably heard of the no-contact rule. It's not a game or a trick to make your ex miss you. It's a period of complete disconnection that allows you to:

- **Regain your sense of self.** Without their influence, you can remember who you are outside the relationship.
- **Break the addiction.** The less you engage, the weaker the craving becomes.
- **Process your emotions.** You need space to feel the grief and begin to heal.
- **Gain perspective.** Distance helps you see the relationship more clearly, including the red flags you might have missed.

## When Texting Your Ex Might Be Okay

There are a few situations where texting might be necessary:

- **Logistical matters:** Returning belongings, settling shared bills, or coordinating co-parenting.
- **Ending on good terms:** If the breakup was mutual and you both want to be friends eventually, a short, kind message after a significant period might be okay.
- **Reconciliation:** If you're both committed to working on issues, a thoughtful message could open a dialogue. But this should only happen after you've done your own healing and are prepared for any outcome.

If you do decide to text, keep it simple, respectful, and without expectations. Avoid emotional outpourings or blame.
