## Should I Text My Ex After a Breakup?

You’re staring at your phone, thumb hovering over their name. The urge to text your ex after a breakup can feel overwhelming—especially when you’re lonely, sad, or replaying memories. But before you hit send, take a breath. Here’s what you need to know.

### Why Your Brain Wants to Text Them

Breakups create a withdrawal-like state. Your brain craves the comfort and dopamine that came from your ex. That’s not weakness—it’s biology. But acting on that urge often leads to more pain.

### 5 Reasons to Wait Before Texting

1. **You’re not thinking clearly.** Emotions cloud judgment. A text sent in desperation can set back weeks of healing.
2. **It can prolong your pain.** Contact keeps the wound open. No contact gives you space to grieve and grow.
3. **You might regret it.** Many people report feeling worse after reaching out—especially if they don’t get the response they hoped for.
4. **It can confuse both of you.** Mixed signals lead to more heartache. Clarity comes from distance.
5. **You deserve to heal first.** Your future self will thank you for choosing self-respect over a temporary fix.

- **Write it out.** Open a notes app or journal and pour out everything you want to say. Don’t send it.

- **Distract yourself.** Go for a walk, call a friend, watch a show, or pick up a hobby. The urge usually passes within 20 minutes.
- **Set a no-contact timer.** Commit to 30 days. Mark it on your calendar. You can always text them after—but you probably won’t want to.

### When It Might Be Okay to Text

There are rare exceptions: if you need to return something important, if you share children, or if you’re both in a calm place and want closure. But even then, ask yourself: *Am I doing this to heal, or to get a reaction?*

### A Word on Safety

If you’re experiencing thoughts of self-harm, depression, or panic, please reach out to a crisis line or mental health professional. Fresh Start is a supportive tool, but it is not a replacement for therapy or emergency care. If you are in an abusive relationship, contact a domestic violence hotline before reaching out to your ex.

The best time to text your ex is when you don’t *need* to. When you’re okay with silence. When you’ve healed enough to handle any outcome. Until then, give yourself the gift of space.
