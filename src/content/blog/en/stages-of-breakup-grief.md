Breakup grief can feel confusing because the person is still alive, but the relationship, routine, future, and version of yourself inside that relationship are gone.

That is real loss.

## Denial: "This cannot be real"

Denial may look like waiting for them to change their mind, rereading old messages, or feeling strangely numb.

Try not to argue with yourself. Gently anchor in facts: "The relationship ended. My body is catching up."

## Anger: "How could they do this?"

Anger can be protective. It reminds you that something hurt. But if it becomes your only fuel, it can keep you emotionally tied to the person.

Use anger to notice boundaries, not to plan revenge.

## Bargaining: "Maybe if I explain it better"

Bargaining is the stage where texting feels most tempting. Your brain tries to find the one sentence, apology, photo, or memory that will reverse the loss.

Before reaching out, ask: am I seeking repair, or am I trying to escape pain?

## Sadness: "I miss my old life"

Sadness is not proof you made the wrong decision or that you will always feel this way. It is proof that something mattered.

Make sadness easier to carry:

- Eat something simple.
- Get daylight.
- Tell one safe person the truth.
- Write what you miss without turning it into a plan to contact them.

## Acceptance: "I can live with what happened"

Acceptance is not liking the breakup. It is no longer needing the breakup to be different before you can take care of yourself.

Some days you will feel accepting. Some days you will feel thrown backward. That movement is normal. Healing is not a straight hallway; it is a return to yourself in pieces.
