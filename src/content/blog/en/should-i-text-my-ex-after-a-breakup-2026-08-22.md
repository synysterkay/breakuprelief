## The Urge to Text: Why It's So Strong

It's 11 p.m. on a Tuesday, and your thumb hovers over the keyboard. You've typed "Hey…" for the tenth time today. Your heart races. Should you text your ex after a breakup?

If you're here, you're probably wrestling with that exact question. The urge to reach out can feel overwhelming—like an itch you must scratch. But before you hit send, take a breath. This guide will help you decide with clarity, not impulse.

## 7 Questions to Ask Yourself Before Texting

### 1. What's My Real Motivation?

Are you texting because you genuinely need to share something important, or because you're hoping they'll say they miss you? Be brutally honest. Most post-breakup texts are driven by anxiety, loneliness, or a desire to control the outcome. None of those are good reasons.

**If you're texting to get a reaction, don't.**

### 2. What Outcome Am I Hoping For?

Imagine their response. What do you want it to be? If you're hoping for a reconciliation or a heartfelt apology, you're setting yourself up for disappointment. You can't control their reaction, and you'll likely end up feeling worse.

### 3. Will This Text Help Me Heal?

Ask yourself: Will sending this text move me forward or set me back? If it's going to trigger a cycle of overthinking, waiting, and obsessing, it's not helping. Healing means focusing on yourself, not on them.

### 4. Am I Using This Text to Avoid My Own Feelings?

Sometimes we text our ex to distract ourselves from pain. It's a way to feel connected without facing the grief. But avoidance only prolongs the hurt. Let yourself feel the feelings instead of outsourcing them to your ex.

### 5. Is This About Closure?

Closure is something you give yourself, not something your ex can provide. Waiting for the perfect explanation or apology will keep you stuck. You already have enough information to move on—you just need to accept it.

### 6. Have I Given Myself Enough Time?

The first few weeks after a breakup are the hardest. Your brain is literally in withdrawal—the same areas light up as physical pain. If you're in the early days, give yourself a grace period. Don't make decisions from the height of emotion.

### 7. What Would I Tell My Best Friend?

Imagine your best friend in this exact situation. What would you say to them? You'd probably say, "Don't text them. You deserve better." Take your own advice.

## The No-Contact Rule: Why It Works

No contact isn't about playing games or making your ex jealous. It's about giving yourself space to heal. When you stop reaching out, you break the cycle of hope and disappointment. You start to remember who you are outside of the relationship.

**The 30-day rule** is a common starting point, but the length isn't as important as your commitment. During this time, you're not just avoiding contact—you're actively rebuilding your life.

## What If You Have Practical Reasons to Text?

Sometimes you need to text for logistics: returning a jacket, discussing shared bills, or co-parenting. In these cases, keep it short, factual, and neutral. No emojis, no "I miss you," no paragraphs. Just the facts.

**Example:** "Hi [Name], I found your jacket. I can leave it on the porch Saturday. Let me know if that works."

That's it. No follow-up questions. No small talk.

## What to Do Instead of Texting

When the urge hits, try these alternatives:

- **Write the text in your notes app** and don't send it. Get it out of your system.
- **Call a friend** and tell them how you're feeling.
- **Do a physical activity**—run, stretch, dance—to move the nervous energy.

## Breaking No Contact: What to Do If You Slip Up

So you already texted. It happens. Don't beat yourself up. Here's what to do next:

1. **Acknowledge the slip** – "I texted them, and that's okay. I'm human."
2. **Don't double down** – Resist the urge to send a follow-up if they don't reply.
3. **Learn from it** – What triggered the urge? How can you handle it better next time?
4. **Resume no contact** – Even if they replied, you can still choose to step back and focus on yourself.

## When to Seek Professional Help

Breakups can trigger deep emotional pain. If you're experiencing symptoms of depression, panic attacks, or thoughts of self-harm, it's crucial to reach out to a mental health professional. You deserve support.

> **Safety Note:** If you are in crisis or having thoughts of self-harm, please contact a local crisis line immediately or go to your nearest emergency room. You matter, and help is available.
