# Should I Text My Ex After a Breakup? A Therapist-Approved Guide (2026)

You're lying in bed at 2 AM, phone in hand, staring at their name. A thousand thoughts race: *What if I just say hi? Maybe they miss me too.* The urge to text your ex after a breakup can feel overwhelming. But before you hit send, take a deep breath. Let's walk through this together.

## Why You Want to Text Them (And Why That's Okay)

The impulse to reach out is completely normal. Breakups create a void—your brain craves the comfort, familiarity, and dopamine hits you once got from them. Common reasons people text an ex:
- You miss them
- You want closure
- You hope to get back together
- You're lonely or bored
- You want to prove you're over them (ironic, right?)

Recognizing the urge doesn't mean you have to act on it. In fact, acting on it often backfires.

## The Risks of Texting Your Ex Too Soon

### 1. It Interrupts Your Healing
Every text you send keeps the emotional connection alive. Your brain stays in "maybe" mode instead of accepting the loss. The no-contact rule exists for a reason: it gives your mind space to rewire and detach.

### 2. You Might Regret It
Emotional texts sent in the heat of the moment often lead to embarrassment later. You can't unsend a message that reveals your vulnerabilities to someone who may not handle them with care.

### 3. It Can Delay Moving On
Each reply (or lack thereof) can send you into a spiral of overanalysis. Did they take too long to respond? Why did they use that emoji? This keeps you stuck in the past.

### 4. It May Reopen Wounds
If the relationship was toxic or the breakup was painful, contacting your ex can trigger anxiety, sadness, or even trauma responses. Your emotional safety comes first.

> **Safety Note:** If you are experiencing thoughts of self-harm, severe depression, or panic, please reach out to a crisis helpline (such as 988 in the US) or contact a mental health professional immediately. This article is not a substitute for professional help.

## When (If Ever) Is It Okay to Text an Ex?

There's no universal rule, but here are scenarios where it *might* be acceptable—*after* significant time and healing:
- You need to exchange belongings or handle logistics
- You've both moved on and want to be friends (rarely works, but possible)
- You want to apologize for your part in the breakup (without expecting anything in return)

Even then, ask yourself: *What is my intention? Am I prepared for any response—including silence?*

## Healthier Alternatives to Texting Your Ex

Instead of reaching out to them, try these:
