# Should I Text My Ex After a Breakup?

It’s late at night, your heart feels heavy, and your thumb hovers over their name. The urge to text your ex after a breakup can feel overwhelming—like a survival instinct. But before you hit send, take a deep breath. Here’s what you need to know.

## Why You Want to Text Them

You might be hoping for:
- An apology or explanation
- A sign they still care
- To ease your loneliness or anxiety
- To rekindle the relationship

These feelings are completely normal. But acting on them usually leads to more pain, not healing.

## The Risks of Reaching Out

- **You may feel rejected again** if they don’t reply or respond coldly.
- **You might say something you regret** out of hurt or anger.
- **You could fall back into an unhealthy cycle** of on-again, off-again contact.
- **You slow down your own healing** by reopening fresh wounds.

## The No Contact Rule: Why It Works

No contact isn’t a game—it’s a powerful tool to give yourself space to heal. It helps you:
- Regain emotional stability
- See the relationship clearly
- Build self-worth independent of your ex

If you’re struggling with depression, panic, or thoughts of self-harm, please reach out to a crisis line (e.g., 988 in the US) or a mental health professional. **This article is not a substitute for professional help.**

## When (If Ever) Is It Okay to Text?

There are very few good reasons to text an ex after a breakup:
- To return something important (and only if no other option exists)
- To discuss shared responsibilities (like bills or pets)
- After months of healing, if you both want to reconnect as friends

Even then, keep it brief, neutral, and don’t expect a reply.
