## Should I Text My Ex After a Breakup?

It's late, your phone is in your hand, and your thumb hovers over their name. The urge to text your ex can feel overwhelming—especially when you're lonely, confused, or missing the comfort of what once was. But before you hit send, take a deep breath. This decision matters, and you deserve a choice that supports your healing.

### Why Do You Want to Text Them?

Ask yourself honestly: *What am I hoping to get from this message?* Common reasons include:

- **Closure** – You want answers or an apology.
- **Loneliness** – You miss the connection and familiarity.
- **Hope** – You think one message could rekindle the relationship.
- **Anger** – You want to confront or hurt them.
- **Guilt** – You feel responsible and want to apologize.

None of these are wrong, but they often lead to more pain. Sending a text when you're emotional rarely brings the relief you're seeking.

### The 5 Questions to Ask Yourself

Before you text, pause and answer these:

1. **Am I calm right now?** If you're angry, sad, or desperate, wait at least 24 hours.
2. **What is my best-case outcome?** Be realistic. Will a reply actually make you feel better?
3. **Can I handle any response (or silence)?** If they don't reply or say something hurtful, are you ready?
4. **Is this about me or them?** Are you trying to soothe your own pain, or genuinely checking on their well-being?
5. **What would I tell a friend in my situation?** Often, we give better advice to others than to ourselves.

If your answers reveal that you're acting from pain or impulse, it's best to wait.

### The No Contact Rule: Why It Helps

No contact isn't a punishment—it's a gift to yourself. It gives your brain time to detach, your heart room to grieve, and your life space to rebuild. The first few weeks are the hardest, but each day without contact strengthens your resolve.

### What to Do Instead of Texting

- **Write it out.** Draft the message in a notes app or journal. You might find you don't need to send it.
- **Talk to someone.** A trusted friend, therapist, or [Fresh Start](https://apps.apple.com/app/fresh-start-breakup-therapy-ai/id6749954260) (iOS) / [Fresh Start](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.breakup.therapy.therapyforabreakup.therapistforbreakups) (Android) can offer private, non-judgmental support.
- **Distract yourself.** Go for a walk, watch a movie, or pick up a hobby. The urge will pass.
- **Practice self-care.** You're going through a loss. Treat yourself with the kindness you'd offer a friend.

### When It Might Be Okay to Text

There are rare, practical reasons to reach out:
- Returning important belongings
- Coordinating logistics (e.g., shared lease)
- A genuine, calm desire to check in after significant time has passed (months, not days)

If you do text, keep it brief, neutral, and respectful. Don't expect a reply, and don't send a follow-up.

### A Note on Safety

If you're experiencing thoughts of self-harm, abuse, or a mental health crisis, please reach out to a professional immediately. Texting an ex is not a solution. Contact a crisis line or your local emergency services. Your safety comes first.
