# How to Stop Checking Your Ex on Social Media: 7 Steps to Break the Cycle

You know you shouldn't do it. But your thumb seems to move on its own, typing their name into the search bar. A quick peek turns into 20 minutes of scrolling, and suddenly you feel worse than before. If this sounds familiar, you're not alone – and you can break free.

## Why We Can't Stop Looking

After a breakup, your brain is in withdrawal. Checking your ex's social media feels like a tiny hit of connection, but it actually keeps the wound open. You compare their highlight reel to your behind-the-scenes pain, and it fuels anxiety, jealousy, and false hope. The good news? You can retrain your brain.

## 7 Practical Steps to Stop Checking

### 1. Remove the Temptation
- **Mute, unfollow, or block** – even temporarily. You don't need to see their posts to heal.
- **Delete the apps** from your phone for a week. Use a browser if you really need them.
- **Turn off notifications** for all social media to reduce triggers.

### 2. Create a Friction Barrier
Make checking harder. Change your password to something random and don't save it. Log out after each use. When the urge hits, the extra steps give you time to pause and choose differently.

### 3. Use the 10-Minute Rule
When you feel the urge, set a timer for 10 minutes. Often the urge passes.

### 4. Name What You're Really Feeling
Ask yourself: *What am I hoping to find?* Usually it's reassurance, closure, or a sign they miss you. None of those come from a screen. Instead, write down your feelings or talk to Fresh Start's AI, which listens without judgment and helps you process.

### 5. Replace the Habit with a Self-Care Ritual
Every time you'd normally check, do something kind for yourself. Make tea, do a breathing exercise, or listen to a podcast. You're rewiring your brain to associate that trigger with self-love, not pain.

### 6. Track Your Progress
Keep a simple tally of how many times you check each day. Just noticing the pattern reduces the behavior. Celebrate small wins – even one less check is progress.

## What If You Slip Up?
Forgive yourself. Breaking a habit takes time. Each slip is a learning opportunity. Ask yourself what triggered it and plan for next time. Healing isn't linear.

## When to Seek Professional Help
If checking your ex's social media is causing severe anxiety, depression, or interfering with daily life, consider talking to a therapist. **Safety note:** If you experience thoughts of self-harm, panic attacks, or are in an abusive situation, please contact a crisis line or emergency services immediately.
