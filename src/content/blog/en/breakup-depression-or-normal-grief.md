Breakups can make ordinary tasks feel impossible. Eating, showering, sleeping, working, and answering messages may suddenly require effort you do not have.

Some of that can be grief. Some of it may be a sign that you need more support.

## Normal breakup grief can include

- Crying often.
- Missing them intensely.
- Low appetite or comfort eating.
- Trouble sleeping.
- Mood swings.
- Wanting to isolate.
- Feeling waves of anger, panic, or regret.

These symptoms can be painful without automatically meaning something is clinically wrong.

## Get extra support if you notice

- You cannot function for days at a time.
- You feel hopeless most of the day.
- You stop eating or sleeping in a dangerous way.
- You use alcohol, drugs, or risky behavior to numb the pain.
- You feel like life is not worth living.
- You have thoughts of harming yourself.

If you might hurt yourself or are in immediate danger, contact emergency services or a crisis line now.

## What helps in the meantime

Shrink the day. Do not ask yourself to rebuild your life by Friday.

Try:

- One glass of water.
- One shower.
- One text to a safe person.
- One meal with protein.
- One short walk.
- One honest journal entry.

Small care counts when your system is overloaded.

## You are not "too much"

Breakup pain can carry rejection, attachment loss, identity disruption, and loneliness all at once. Needing support does not mean you are failing. It means the loss is heavy, and heavy things should not always be carried alone.
