The no-contact rule is not about pretending you do not care. It is about giving your nervous system a break from the person who currently activates the most pain, hope, anger, and longing in you.

For the first 30 days, your goal is not to feel amazing. Your goal is to stop reopening the same wound.

## Days 1-3: remove the easiest triggers

The first days are usually about friction. Make texting, checking, and rereading harder.

- Archive the chat instead of rereading it.
- Mute or hide their stories.
- Move photos into a hidden folder.
- Ask one trusted friend not to update you about them.
- Write one sentence you can repeat: "Contact will give me a spike, not peace."

This is not dramatic. It is basic emotional first aid.

## Days 4-10: expect withdrawal energy

Missing someone can feel like urgency. You may feel convinced that one message will calm everything. Before you send it, ask:

- What feeling am I trying to escape?
- What do I hope they will say?
- If they do not respond the way I want, will I feel better or worse?

If the honest answer is "worse," wait.

## Days 11-20: build replacement rituals

No contact leaves empty space. If you do not fill it intentionally, your mind will fill it with checking and guessing.

Create a breakup routine:

- Morning: one small body reset, like a walk or stretch.
- Afternoon: one task that restores competence.
- Night: one private journal entry instead of scrolling.

Fresh Start can guide these check-ins when you do not know what to write.

## Days 21-30: decide from calm, not panic

By the end of 30 days, you may still miss them. That does not mean no contact failed. It means you are human.

Ask yourself:

- Am I clearer than I was on day one?
- Do I want contact, or do I want relief?
- Would contact support my healing, or restart the loop?

If there was abuse, manipulation, stalking, or fear, prioritize safety and professional support. No contact may need to become a long-term boundary.

## What if you need to communicate?

Shared housing, children, pets, bills, or belongings can require limited contact. Keep it short, practical, and written. Do not use logistics as a doorway into emotional processing.

The 30-day no-contact rule works best when it is not treated as a trick to get your ex back. Treat it as a way to get yourself back.
