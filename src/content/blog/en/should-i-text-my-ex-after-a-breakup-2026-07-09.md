## Should I Text My Ex After a Breakup?

It's 2 AM, you're lying in bed, and your thumb hovers over their name. Should you text your ex? The urge is real, and you're not alone. Millions of people ask themselves this question every day. Let's break it down with compassion and practical advice.

## The Short Answer

In most cases, **no, you should not text your ex**—at least not yet. The first weeks and months after a breakup are emotionally charged. Texting often leads to more pain, confusion, or false hope. But there are exceptions, and we'll cover those.

## Why You Want to Text

Your brain is in withdrawal. Relationships release dopamine and oxytocin, and when they end, your brain craves that hit. Texting feels like a quick fix, but it's usually a Band-Aid on a deeper wound. Common reasons:

- **Loneliness** – You miss the companionship.
- **Closure** – You want answers to 'why'.
- **Hope** – You think one message can fix everything.
- **Habit** – You used to share everything with them.

None of these are bad reasons, but they're all about *your* need, not what's best for your healing.

## The No Contact Rule

The 'No Contact Rule' isn't a game—it's a healing tool. It gives you space to grieve, rediscover yourself, and break the emotional addiction. Experts recommend at least 30-60 days of no contact. During this time:

- Block or mute their social media.
- Delete their number temporarily (or use an app to hide it).
- Write letters you never send.
- Talk to a trusted friend or an AI coach like **Fresh Start**.

## When *Is* It Okay to Text?

There are a few scenarios where texting might be appropriate:

1. **Logistics** – You need to exchange belongings, settle bills, or handle shared responsibilities. Keep it brief and businesslike.
2. **Genuine Apology** – If you hurt them and haven't apologized, a single, sincere message can be okay. Don't expect a reply.
3. **Shared Crisis** – A serious emergency (illness, death, etc.) warrants a brief, compassionate message.
4. **After Significant Healing** – Months later, if you're both in a good place and want to reconnect as friends or more, a casual check-in can work. But proceed slowly.

## The Danger of Texting Too Soon

Texting too soon can:

- **Set back your healing** – Their reply (or silence) can reopen wounds.
- **Create false hope** – A friendly reply doesn't mean they want to get back together.
- **Push them away** – They may feel pressured or annoyed.
- **Lower your self-worth** – Chasing someone who isn't choosing you hurts your confidence.

## What to Do Instead of Texting

When the urge strikes, try these:

- **Journal** – Write everything you want to say. Get it out of your head.
- **Move your body** – A walk, run, or yoga session can shift your mood.
