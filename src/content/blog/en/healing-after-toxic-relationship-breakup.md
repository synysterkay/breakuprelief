Leaving a toxic relationship does not always feel like freedom right away. Sometimes it feels like withdrawal. You may miss the person, the intensity, the apology phase, or the version of them you kept hoping would stay.

That does not mean leaving was wrong.

## Why toxic breakups are confusing

Toxic relationships often mix pain with reward. There may have been chemistry, deep apologies, intense closeness, and moments that felt perfect.

Your brain can become attached to the relief after conflict, not just the person.

That is why you can know something was unhealthy and still crave it.

## Keep a reality list

When nostalgia edits the relationship, write down reality:

- What happened that I do not want to repeat?
- How did my body feel in the relationship?
- What did I keep explaining away?
- What did I lose touch with in myself?

Read this list when you want to go back.

## Rebuild trust in yourself

Toxic dynamics can make you doubt your memory, standards, and intuition. Healing includes proving to yourself that you can protect your peace now.

Start with small promises:

- I will not respond tonight.
- I will not check their profile.
- I will tell the truth to one safe person.
- I will not minimize what hurt me.

## Safety comes first

If the relationship involved abuse, threats, stalking, coercion, or fear, make a safety plan with trusted people or professional services. Do not rely on an app alone for dangerous situations.

Healthy love should not require you to abandon your nervous system to keep the connection.
