Rumination is when your mind keeps circling the same breakup questions:

What did I miss? Did they ever love me? Could I have saved it? Are they happier? Will they come back?

It feels productive because your brain is searching for the missing piece. But after a certain point, the loop stops giving insight and starts draining you.

## Rumination is not reflection

Reflection leads somewhere. Rumination repeats.

Reflection asks, "What did I learn about my needs?" Rumination asks, "What if I had said one different sentence?"

Reflection leaves you clearer. Rumination leaves you more activated.

## Give the loop a container

Set a 15-minute worry window. During that time, write everything uncensored. When the timer ends, write:

"I have given this thought time. I can return tomorrow."

Then do something physical: walk, shower, stretch, clean one surface, or make food.

The body shift matters because rumination lives partly in your nervous system, not just your thoughts.

## Ask better questions

Instead of:

"Why wasn't I enough?"

Try:

"What did this relationship repeatedly make me feel?"

Instead of:

"How do I get them back?"

Try:

"What version of me am I afraid of becoming without them?"

Better questions do not erase pain, but they move you toward yourself.

## When the thought returns

It will return. That does not mean the technique failed.

Say: "This is the loop again." Naming it creates a little distance. Then choose one action that belongs to your life, not the relationship: laundry, food, work, sunlight, a friend, a journal, or rest.

You do not need to stop thinking about your ex completely. You need your thoughts to stop owning the whole day.
