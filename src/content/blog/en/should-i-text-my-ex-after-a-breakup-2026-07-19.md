## Should I Text My Ex After a Breakup?

Breakups are messy. One moment you're sure you never want to see them again, the next you're staring at their contact, thumb hovering over the message icon. It's completely human to wonder: *Should I text my ex?*

Before you type a single word, take a breath. This guide will help you think it through with kindness for yourself.

### The No-Contact Rule: Why It Works

No contact isn't about punishment—it's about giving your brain space to heal. After a breakup, your emotions are raw, and your judgment is clouded. Texting your ex often leads to:

- **Reopening wounds** – Seeing their name pop up can trigger a flood of pain.
- **Mixed signals** – A friendly reply might give false hope, while silence can hurt more.
- **Losing progress** – Every day of no contact helps you detach. A single text can reset the clock.

**Tip:** If you're struggling with the urge, try writing the message in a notes app instead of sending it. Then close the app and do something grounding—like a short walk or a few deep breaths.

### What Are You Hoping to Achieve?

Ask yourself honestly: *Why do I want to text them?*

- **Closure?** Closure comes from within, not from your ex. You can give yourself closure by accepting the relationship is over.
- **Reconciliation?** If you want to get back together, rushing rarely works. Both people need time to reflect and change.
- **To stop the pain?** Texting your ex might provide a temporary hit of relief, but it often prolongs the heartache. The pain will pass if you let it.

### When It Might Be Okay to Text Your Ex

There are a few exceptions where reaching out could be appropriate:

- **Logistical matters** – You need to return belongings or finalize shared responsibilities. Keep it brief and businesslike.
- **Safety concerns** – If you're worried about their well-being (e.g., they were struggling with mental health), a short check-in is okay. But don't use this as an excuse to reconnect emotionally.
- **Genuine friendship after healing** – This only works after both of you have fully moved on and have no romantic feelings. That usually takes months or longer.

**Important:** If your relationship was toxic or abusive, do not text your ex. Reach out to a trusted friend, a therapist, or a support hotline instead. Your safety comes first.

### What If You're Feeling Desperate or Depressed?

It's common to feel overwhelming sadness, anxiety, or even hopelessness after a breakup. If you're experiencing thoughts of self-harm or feel like you can't cope, please reach out for help immediately:

- **National Suicide Prevention Lifeline:** 988 (US)
- **Crisis Text Line:** Text HOME to 741741
- **Emergency services:** 911 (US) or your local emergency number
