## Breakup Anxiety at Night: What Helps Right Now

Nighttime can be the hardest part of a breakup. The world goes quiet, your phone stops buzzing, and suddenly you're alone with your thoughts. Your heart races, your mind replays conversations, and sleep feels impossible. If you're reading this at 2 a.m., you're not alone—and there are things you can do right now to find calm.

### Why Nighttime Makes Anxiety Worse

After a breakup, your brain is in overdrive. Daytime distractions—work, friends, errands—keep the pain at bay. But at night, there's nothing to distract you. The darkness and silence can amplify feelings of loss and uncertainty. Your stress hormone cortisol may also spike, making it hard to relax.

### What Helps Right Now

**1. Ground Yourself**

Use the 5-4-3-2-1 technique: Look around and name 5 things you see, 4 you can touch, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, and 1 you taste. This pulls your brain out of the past and into the present.

**2. Breathe Slowly**

Try the 4-7-8 method: Inhale through your nose for 4 seconds, hold for 7 seconds, exhale through your mouth for 8 seconds. Repeat 4 times. This activates your parasympathetic nervous system.

**3. Write It Out**

Grab a notebook or open your phone's notes app. Write down everything swirling in your head—fears, regrets, anger, sadness. Don't edit, just release. Once it's on paper, it has less power over you.

**5. Create a Sleep Ritual**

Dim the lights, avoid screens 30 minutes before bed, and do something soothing: read a gentle book, listen to calming music, or take a warm bath. Consistency signals your brain that it's safe to rest.

**6. Challenge Catastrophic Thoughts**

Anxiety often whispers worst-case scenarios. Ask yourself: "Is this thought 100% true? What's a more balanced way to see this?" You can't control the future, but you can control how you respond to tonight.

If nighttime anxiety is accompanied by thoughts of self-harm, panic attacks, or feelings of hopelessness, please reach out to a professional. You can call or text 988 (US) for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. Fresh Start is a supportive tool, but it's not a replacement for therapy or emergency care.

Breakup anxiety at night feels endless, but it's temporary. Each small step you take—a deep breath, a grounding exercise, a chat with Fresh Start—builds resilience. Be gentle with yourself. Healing isn't linear, and tonight's struggle doesn't define your tomorrow.
