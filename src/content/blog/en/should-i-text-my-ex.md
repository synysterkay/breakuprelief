The urge to text your ex can feel urgent because your nervous system is asking for relief. It may not be asking for the relationship back. It may be asking for comfort, certainty, apology, distraction, or proof that you still matter.

Before you send anything, pause and ask one cleaner question: what am I hoping this message will make me feel?

## If you want reassurance

Texting can briefly lower anxiety, but it often teaches your brain that contact is the only way to feel safe. Try getting reassurance from a safer source first: a friend, a journal, a grounding exercise, or an AI breakup support check-in inside Fresh Start.

## If you want closure

Closure rarely arrives in one perfect message. If the breakup is fresh, your mind may keep moving the finish line. Write the message privately and wait until tomorrow. If it still feels clear, respectful, and necessary after sleep, you can decide again.

## If you are in no contact

No contact is not a punishment. It is a boundary that gives your body time to stop treating every notification like a crisis. When the urge hits, make the next ten minutes your only job.

Try this:

- Put the phone across the room.
- Write the exact message without sending it.
- Name the emotion in one sentence.
- Do one physical reset: water, shower, walk, breathing, or stretching.
- Recheck the urge after ten minutes.

## When to get extra help

If breakup pain comes with thoughts of harming yourself, feeling unsafe, or being unable to function, reach out to local emergency services, a crisis line, or a licensed mental health professional. Support apps can help with daily coping, but immediate danger needs immediate human help.
