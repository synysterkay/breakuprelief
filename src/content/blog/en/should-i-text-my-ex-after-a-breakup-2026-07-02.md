## Should I Text My Ex After a Breakup?

You're lying in bed at 2 a.m., phone in hand, thumb hovering over the keyboard. The urge to text your ex is overwhelming. You miss them. You wonder if they miss you too. Maybe one message could fix everything.

Before you hit send, take a breath. Let's walk through this together.

### Why You Want to Text Your Ex

The urge to reach out is completely normal. After a breakup, your brain is in withdrawal—literally. Love activates the same reward centers as addictive substances. When that bond breaks, your brain craves the "hit" of connection.

Common reasons you might want to text:
- **Loneliness**: You miss the daily check-ins and inside jokes.
- **Closure**: You feel like you need one more conversation to understand.
- **Hope**: You believe things could be different this time.
- **Habit**: Your thumb just knows their name in your favorites.

### 5 Questions to Ask Yourself Before Texting

#### 1. What outcome am I hoping for?
Be honest. If you want to get back together, ask yourself: Has anything really changed? Are the issues that caused the breakup resolved? If not, a text won't fix them.

#### 2. Am I emotionally regulated right now?
If you're sad, angry, or desperate, you're not in a state to communicate clearly. Wait until you feel calm and centered.

#### 3. Will this text help me heal or set me back?
Texting often reopens the wound. It can trigger anxiety while you wait for a reply, and disappointment if they don't respond how you hoped.

#### 4. What would I tell a friend in my shoes?
If your best friend was about to text their ex, what would you say? Probably: "Give it more time." Listen to that voice.

#### 5. Is there a healthier way to express this?
Instead of texting your ex, try:
- Writing a letter you never send
- Recording a voice memo to yourself

### When It Might Be Okay to Text

There are a few situations where reaching out is reasonable:
- **Returning belongings**: "I have your jacket. Let me know where to drop it off."
- **Logistics**: If you share children or pets, practical communication is necessary.
- **Genuine closure**: After months of space, if you feel truly neutral and just want to apologize or thank them, it can be healing—but only if you have no expectations.

### The No Contact Rule: Your Best Friend

No contact means no calls, texts, social media, or any form of communication for a set period (usually 30-60 days). It gives you:
- Space to grieve without interruption
- Time to rediscover who you are without them
- A chance to break the addiction loop

### What to Do Instead of Texting

1. **Write it out**: Open a notes app and type everything you want to say. Don't send it.
2. **Talk to someone**: Call a friend, or use Fresh Start's AI companion to vent 24/7.
3. **Move your body**: A walk, run, or yoga class can shift your energy.
4. **Delete the thread**: Archive or delete the conversation so you're not tempted to re-read old messages.

### A Gentle Reminder

If you're feeling intense despair, hopelessness, or thoughts of self-harm, please reach out to a crisis line or a mental health professional. This article is not a substitute for therapy. Texting your ex won't cure heartbreak, but taking care of yourself will.

### Final Thought

The urge to text your ex is a sign that you're human. But you don't have to act on every urge. Give yourself the gift of space. The person you're meant to be on the other side of this healing is worth protecting.
