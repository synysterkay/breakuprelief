## Introduction

Breakups are hard. And in 2026, the question "Should I text my ex?" still haunts many of us. You might feel lonely, regretful, or just curious. Before you hit send, take a breath. This article will help you decide with clarity and self-compassion.

## Why You Want to Text Your Ex

First, ask yourself: *Why do I want to reach out?* Common reasons include:
- Missing them
- Wanting closure
- Hoping to get back together
- Feeling guilty or sorry for them
- Just wanting to break the silence

None of these are wrong, but they can lead to more pain if you act impulsively.

## The No Contact Rule: Still Relevant in 2026

No contact isn't a game—it's a healing tool. By not texting, calling, or checking social media, you give your brain time to rewire. Studies show that 30–60 days of no contact can reduce emotional dependency and help you see the relationship more clearly.

**When to break no contact:**
- You need to return important belongings
- You share children or pets
- You're genuinely ready to be friends (after months of healing)

**When to stay silent:**
- You're angry, sad, or desperate
- You hope texting will fix things
- You're drunk or lonely late at night

## What to Text If You Decide To

If you're sure, keep it simple. Avoid paragraphs, blame, or "I miss you." Examples:
- "Hi, I found your charger. Want me to leave it at your mailbox?"
- "I saw your email about the lease—let's figure out a time to talk."

Remember: you can't control their response. Be prepared for silence, a short reply, or even anger.
